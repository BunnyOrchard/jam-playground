# Jam Playground

Jam Playground is a generative music machine for the M-VAVE FM-1. Switch it on and it plays, and three
quiet notes rise with it. Each boot is a different song: a scale, a tempo, a handful of parts, and a form that moves
on its own.

It is a fork of [HYP-1](https://github.com/RustyPawz/fm1-HYP1) by RedtailWorks, which is a fork of
[AMB-1](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-amb) by Charles Vestal, on the Felucca platform by Leo
Kuroshita and Hügelton Instruments. The synth, the drums and the space are theirs. Jam Playground is
the player on top. GPL-3.0-only, same as HYP-1. Their names stay in the tree.

## What you hear

Thirteen vibes. A new song picks one of the first twelve at random. The PRESETS dial steps through
all thirteen, and the one you land on is already that music. Each vibe holds eight songs. Turn the
same vibe again and the opening is another of those eight: another Felucca sound, another kit,
another beat in the same family, another noise. A new song on any vibe but the opening drum and
bass draws from those eight too. The row below is the recipe, and it is still one of them. Sleep
is the last. One more step, and the dial is back at the start.

| Vibe | Somewhere near | What you hear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dnb | 170, dorian, 9ths | a dark half-time beat, a reese, a singing pad |
| Reggae | 82, mixolydian, dub chords | a one-drop, an organ, a sub, an offbeat chop, dub echo |
| Trance | 124, aeolian, suspended chords | a steady kick, a hat, a soft acid line |
| Still | 73, major pentatonic, fourths | a long chord, a warm drone, a low tone breathing in the room |
| Disco | 118, mixolydian, sevenths | a four-on-the-floor, sixteenth hats, a warm bass, a 909 |
| Funk | 98, dorian, 9ths | a swung pocket, stabs, a sub, an 808 |
| Electro | 114, phrygian, suspended chords | an FM bass, bleeps, a dry 808 |
| House | 122, ionian, sevenths | an organ, a 909, the hat on the offbeat |
| Hip hop | 90, aeolian, suspended chords | a break, a swung hat, a dark tone under the chord |
| Garage | 132, mixolydian, 9ths | a two-step kick, a heavy swing, a house kit |
| Dubstep | 140, phrygian, fourths | half time, a reese that wobbles, a hard kit |
| Wave | 100, aeolian, suspended chords | strings, a pulse bass and an acid line about twenty lower, a long echo, an 808 |
| Sleep | 56, major pentatonic, suspended chords | wind and birds, a drone that leaves, a flute, and now and then a bell |

A recipe is a few hundred bytes, and the image has room for many more. The twelve dance vibes are
as many as one pad, one bass, one acid and four air parts keep apart. Sleep is the thirteenth, on
the dial after Wave. It uses those same engines, played slowly: the fourth part is wind, the drone
sings, and the acid line is a flute. A new song still picks among the twelve. The ten numbered pieces
under GLO are a different bank. That save fits fourteen.

A song from the dial starts in the groove. Power-on, and ENV, open on a smaller spine and let the
form arrive there. ENV waits for the next bar, then crosses with one of four moves, and each
press picks another: a cue loop of the song you are leaving, a delay throw, a stutter, or a
reverse. The PRESETS dial waits for that same bar and crosses the same way into the vibe you
land on. The new song is already playing underneath. About a third of the parts are live on that opening. The others are
loaded and silent, so a click brings them in already in tune on the next bar, including
through the intro and the break. The next section folds the levels back into the arrangement. The
parts you left on stay on. PLAY is the conductor, and it is on from the moment the machine starts.
It walks an intro, a groove, a lift, a break, a drop and a glide, then around again. Break swaps
the arrangement: two parts that are playing step out, and two that are quiet step in. SAVE
crosses into that break the way a new song crosses, a cue, a throw, a stutter, or a reverse,
and the next press picks a different one. The break is already playing underneath. The form's
own break does not cross. Drop sits
one part out, or two when several are already playing, for one bar, and then those parts come
back. The rest of the song stays. It does
not shut the filter down to a drone. A button you press holds the form for eight bars. The drop
lets go after that one bar. Press PLAY again and the screen says MANUAL: the music keeps
going, and the form waits. AUTO stays on the left while it conducts, with the section name beside it.

The title row is a clock: sixteen marks for this bar, in four beats, and four marks for
which bar of the phrase. A click, a sound, a kit, break, excite, a pump, drop, back, a new
song, and the vibe dial wait for the next bar. Lets Find Out throws the last beat across
the song as you press it, and the song stays loud. The place it finds waits for that bar:
a new chord, a new part brought up, a busier beat, or the air brought forward. Sleep adds
a blur, then steps into the next room. That wait is the quantize. Level, shape,
cutoff, tempo, the scale, effects, and PLAY happen as you touch them.

The pad sings with the bar. Its four voices share a clock of two bars, and that shape
changes: a line, a late bloom, a short chord, then a quieter answer. They come in one
after another, and one of them sometimes sits out, so it is not the same block again.
A new note comes every four bars. A new section brings a new chord and a new sound.
After that, the pad leaves the style's four sounds and a drum pattern steps with it.
The intro holds the sound it opened on. A break holds the colour it arrived on. Turn the
pad's sound or cutoff and the conductor leaves those alone for eight bars.
With PLAY off, the pad stays where you set it. Stabs, dub, pings, hits and rave
still play, and they sit at half the level of a singing pad, so the hit does
not take the song. Glass sits at 32% of that singing level. The stored level
and the percent stay, and the other singing sounds stay. Turning the pad up
still raises them.

The acid comes on at 48. The bass hits and leaves. It speaks once a bar (once every two bars on Still, once every eight
on Sleep) and the note decays, so it does not hold under the whole chord. Electro is the exception that keeps a song
under the line: the acid plays a bleep riff in one octave, an octave up, and the bass and the
808 go with it. A new song starts on that line with the kick and the hat. The groove brings
the bass, the backbeat and the dust in underneath, and each section opens the acid filter a
step. The pad stays out, so the line can lead. A break lets the bass and the dust step back.
A drop sits that backing out and leaves the line playing.

The sound is HYP-1's: one pad, one drone, one acid line, a fourth part, and six drum lanes. Five
more drums sit beside those lanes, so the song screen has one drum for every black key. The
fourth part follows the vibe. Drum and bass, electro and
dubstep play Dust, a shaker on the off-beats, tuned to the chord. Reggae, funk and garage play Chop, a short chord on the
offbeat, in the song's key. Trance, disco, house and wave play Air, a soft tone pulsing on the eighths. Still and hip hop
play Hush, a low tone breathing in the room. Sleep plays Wind: a breeze, a little water, and a bird
now and then. Each engine has sixteen steps. Dust opens on those off-beats and a chop on the & of
each beat. Pad, bass, acid, air, hush and wind open on every step, so an untouched song stays as it
was. Hold the key and tap SEQ and the step screen edits that row. A click of FX while the key is held mutates that row. Its drone is throat singing that swells and leaves. Its acid line is a flute. On the
break the hat is a wind chime and the perc is a singing bowl. OCT+ on Sleep does not add drums or
open the filters. Knob 1 walks pink, wood and vinyl. Wood is a short knock on the chord,
an octave under the pink shaker. Hold the key and knob 2 leans that key's notes up or down the scale, the same phrase as on the sequence screen, heard as you turn.
Knob 4 is the tone, heard at once. On Dust a higher tone opens the grain and shortens the shake. On wood it opens the fifth and shortens the knock. There are twenty
sounds for the pad, seventeen for the bass, twenty for the acid, and ten drum kits. FM piano, vibes, kalimba and harp are sampled pads. The upright is a sampled bass. Flute, tenor sax, muted trumpet and harmonica are sampled acid sounds. Dusty is a sampled kit. A new song draws those with the others. Knob 1 walks the whole list, one step a turn, and SEL with ALGORITHM wraps it. Sleep stays on its own oscillators. Each engine plays one
sound. Each named key is a further voice on that engine's note. Four of the engines
and those sounds can be on at once. A fifth turns the oldest of them off, and the new
one stays. The drums are not part of that count. Each drum has its own kit. A song opens with the clap on when the snare is,
and the open hat on when the hat is. Auto brings an untouched clap back with the snare, and an
untouched open hat back with the hat. A key still moves only its own drum. With nothing held, ALGORITHM turns
the scale. Holding a key, or with the sequence screen up, it mutates that track and the scale waits. The keys do not.

## The panel

White keys, left to right: **Pad, Bass, Acid**, then **Dust, Chop, Air, Hush or Wind**, then twelve sounds.
**Glass, Dark, Dub, Stabs** are pad sounds. **Sub, Reese, Hoover, FM** are bass sounds. **Pluck, Trance,
Bleep, Soft** are acid sounds. Each key is that one sound, and a click adds it. The pad keeps
playing, and Glass plays the pad's first note beside it. The same is true of the bass and Sub,
and of the acid and Pluck. Four of those sixteen synth keys can be on together. When
another comes on, the one that has been on the longest turns off, on the same bar. Each
named key is one note, not a second chord. Glass is heard at 32% of the pad's level. In House, Pluck comes in
forty lower than that acid's level. The other acid sounds stay.

The engine key stays lit while that part is in the song, and lights fully while its sound is in
the air. A named key stays lit while that voice is on, and lights fully while it is loud. A kick
flash is the kick key, and a bleep flash is Bleep. Between notes the key goes dim again.

The song screen is a grid. The eleven drum slots sit above the sixteen synth slots.
Each drum is one black key, each name once. The synths are one per white key: Pad, Bass,
Acid, and the fourth part, then the twelve sounds.
Bright is in the song. Dim is waiting. A change that is waiting for the bar, or the key you
are holding, shows in the same colour as a held key. Above the grid: the vibe and the tempo,
the bar and the step, Auto or Manual and the section, and the action in progress. Hold a key
and that middle line is the key's name, with the sound or the kit under it.

Black keys, low to high: **kick, snare, clap, hat, open hat, perc, rim, tom, cowbell, shaker,
crash**. Each key is its own kit. A click turns that drum on or off, and only that drum. A white
key turns only that synth. Hold a drum and knob 1 walks its kit: Hypno, House, Techno, Acid,
Break, Minimal, Hard, 808, 909, Dusty. The crash steps over Hypno, House, Acid, Hard, and 808. Those
cymbals are not sounds, and the other drums in those kits stay. The clap does not walk the snare's kit. The open hat does not
walk the hat's. The steps stay in one beat until that drum is mutated or its steps are written: the clap and the rim with
the snare, the open hat and the shaker with the hat, the tom and the cowbell with the perc, the
crash with the kick. A song starts there, and auto leads an untouched drum back into it. A click of FX
on that drum takes its steps. A key you have already pressed stays on or off through later sections.

- A click of a white key lands on the next bar. Pad, Bass and Acid toggle the song's own sound
  for that engine. Glass, Sub, Pluck and the other named keys each add that voice, and the engine
  stays. The same key again turns that voice off. Two clicks in one bar cancel. Four
  synth keys is the limit. The oldest one turns off when a new one comes on. Drums do not
  count toward it. The fourth key
  is dust, chop, air, hush or wind.
  A click of a drum key turns that drum on or off. Let go without turning a knob. Holding longer, and
  letting go without a twist, leaves it so you can read it.
- Hold a key. Knob 1 is its sound, or the kit when the key is a drum, and that one lands on the next bar.
  Knob 2 leans that key's notes up or down the scale, the same phrase as knob 2 on the sequence screen, heard as you turn, and it does not switch the key on.
  The rhythm stays. ALGORITHM mutates that key's track, the same way it does on the sequence screen. The scale waits, and the key does not switch on.
  Turning a step on or off is the sequence screen: select the track, and the white keys do it.
  A named key leans the engine it plays. The sound stays where knob 1 left it.
  While the key is held, the screen names that key, and under the name
  the sound or the kit knob 1 is on. That name moves as you turn, before the bar. The pattern is
  still drawn: a drum shows its sixteen steps, and so does a white key. On a drum, the marked
  step starts at step 1. While the drum key is held, OCT− turns that step on or off, and OCT+ moves
  the mark to the next step. You hear it as you edit. SEQ opens every track that is on.
  SELECT moves through those tracks. OCT− and OCT+ shift the selected track by an octave.
  SEL solos that track, and SEL again brings the rest of the song back. ALGORITHM mutates
  that track, one step a turn, and the scale waits until the screen closes. Knob 1 rotates
  its sound, or its kit when the row is a drum. The title names the one it is aiming at, and
  that sound lands on the next bar. Knob 2 leans each hit of that track up or down the scale, a small phrase that
  opens as you turn and flattens the other way, heard as you turn, and only on that track. The rhythm stays. Knob 3
  is its level. Knob 4 is that track's filter, or that drum's tune. The title shows the stored level. The white keys are that track's steps, lit where it plays.
  A press turns the step. SEQ, GLO, or HOME leaves, and a solo leaves with it. HOME leaves the chord where it is.
  A hold on its own still lets go. Let go without SEQ, and OCT− and OCT+ are back and excite again.
  A written pattern stays through a section, a pump, and
  the link. A click of FX on that drum steps its pattern now, and that step leaves the linked beat.
  A step you write does the same. Knob 3 is its level and knob 4 its cutoff, and those move at once.
  While knob 3 turns, the screen shows that level as a percent. FROM SILENT stays on the same line when the part was dark.
  On a dark key, turn knob 3 up and that part switches on from silence: how far you turn is how
  loud it comes in. Turn knob 3 down and the key stays dark. On a drum, knob 3 is that drum's
  level, and turning it up wakes only that drum. Knob 4 tunes only the drum you hold.
  With the sequence screen closed and no key held, the four knobs are live. Knob 1 is a ring: the song
  already playing is multiplied by a tone, so it grows metallic. Further
  up, that tone is faster and the dry song steps back. At zero the ring
  is shut, and the song keeps the room it baked. Knob 2 is a wah: a
  resonant band rocks through that same song, and the peak grits as the
  knob rises. At zero the wah is shut. Neither writes the plate or the
  echo. Knob 3 is a phaser: the song sweeps through
  a slow comb, wider and a little faster as the knob rises. At zero
  the phaser is shut. All three are dry at zero. Knob 4 alone
  opens every synth filter together, and the four settings show beside
  CUTOFF as you turn. A click of FX with a key held mutates that key, now: the pad's colour and its sixteen,
  the bass sound, the acid line, the fourth key's pattern, or that one drum's pattern.
  A named key mutates the engine it plays, and the screen says the key's name.
  Nothing held mutates the song: a drum pattern, the
  acid line, the pad, the bass, the air, the filters, a kit, or the room.
  The pad, the bass, the acid and the air each move their sixteen steps too.
  Sleep, with nothing held, walks the space, the room, the drone, and the wind.
  It does not move the tempo, and it does not switch a part on or off.
  A held key still owns all four knobs. Holding FX does not move them.
  A turn while FX is down is ignored, and letting go after that turn does not also mutate.
- The song stays on the screen. Tap SEL for a tip, and tap it again for the next. Twenty-seven tips
  walk every control: the keys, the drums and kits, the knobs and the pattern picture, from-silent,
  PLAY, each scene, the vibe dial, Lets Find Out, freeze, the chord, GLO, tempo, scale, FX, and the clock.
  The last tap returns to the song, and so does any other button.
- A twist of sound or cutoff wakes a sleeping part at its level. Level, turned up, is the quiet way in.
- Hold SEL with a part key and that part solos until you let go.
- SELECT is tempo. ALGORITHM is the scale. Holding a key, ALGORITHM mutates that key's track. While the sequence screen is up, SELECT walks the tracks and ALGORITHM mutates the selected one. PRESETS is the vibe dial: dark drum and bass, reggae,
  study trance, still, then disco, funk, electro, house, hip hop, garage, dubstep, wave and sleep,
  and around again. Turn it the other way to go back. It lands on the next bar, and the way across
  is a cue, a throw, a stutter, or a reverse, a different one each turn. A new song does not land
  on Sleep. You dial it.
- MASTER is the volume, as on the hardware. It is not a performance knob.

## Try it here

The host simulator builds with Python and a C compiler. No FM-1 required.

```
host/build_host.sh
build/host/amb_host tests/scenarios/iam.amb /tmp/iam-out
```

Seed 42 is one dark drum-and-bass song, so the test can hear a known piece. On the hardware the seed is noise
from the MASTER pot, and every power-on is a new one.

## Install

Building a package needs Docker (the JieLi toolchain is linux/amd64) and the steps in `BUILDING.md`.
A development package is `build/amb.fwsc`, identity `FM-1_700`. The release package is `build/amb-1.2.0.fwsc`, identity `FM-1_7666200`. The USB name is Jam Playground FM-1, and the tips say JAM. 1.1.0 stays Jam Playground. The 1.0.0 package still says IAMM-1.

With the FM-1 plugged in and in Felucca update mode (hold OCT− and OCT+ for 5 seconds):

```
python3 tools/fm1_install.py build/amb-1.2.0.fwsc
```

Installing replaces whatever firmware is on the unit. To put M-VAVE's firmware back, use the
installer "Back to stock", M-UPGRADE, or, if the chip is sitting in update mode:

```
bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/main/tools/fm1_rescue.sh)"
```

An FM-1 that fails to start twice comes up in a safe mode the installer can reach.
